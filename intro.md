What I Built

Last year, I stood on the steps of a ghat in Varanasi as the evening aarti began. The bells rang, the fire rose, and all around me people knelt at the edge of the Ganga to set small leaf-boat diyas on the water. It’s called deepdaan, the offering of a lamp. I floated one too, with a wish I’ve never said out loud to anyone. I watched it drift until I couldn’t tell which flame was mine. Nobody around me tried to pull theirs back.

They say the Ganga takes whatever you’re ready to release, and nothing you’re still holding.

But the ghat is far. Some nights you’re awake at 2 AM with something heavy: a goodbye you never got to say, someone you miss, words you can’t tell anyone. There’s no river. There’s just a laptop and a webcam.

MannDaan (मन दान, “an offering from the heart”) brings the river to your desk. It isn’t a replacement for deepdaan or the Ganga. It’s a small personal ritual, inspired by what I felt on those steps, for the nights you can’t get there. It:

Asks for consent before anything. The camera stays off until you say yes.
Writes your worry onto a diya sitting at the edge of a ghat at night.
Listens to your breath through the camera. Breathing live from the Presage SmartSpectra SDK drives the flame: rush it and the flame flickers and nearly dies, slow down and it stands tall and still. Your pulse sets the shimmer of the water and the distant bells.
Refuses if you’re still holding on. Breathe too fast and the current pushes your diya back to the ghat: “Ganga doesn’t take what you’re still holding.”
Lets go after seven calm breaths. The diya drifts downstream and your words dissolve into the water like ink.
Keeps nothing. Your words are never written to disk, and no video is recorded. Nothing was saved. That was the point.
